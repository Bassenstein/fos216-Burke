01 · The system

One campus, one closed loop.

Every zone feeds another. The lake supplies water for hydrogen and cooling, city waste becomes feedstock instead of landfill, turbine exhaust becomes district heat, and the power island carries the compute. Nothing leaves the site that it could use.

Swipe the diagram sideways to see all four columns →

Water & hydrogenPower & heatWaste to feedstockHydrogen co-fire back into the turbines
Power

GE Vernova 7HA.03 combined cycle

Heat-recovery steam on every unit, hydrogen co-fire capable, with a reserved parcel for a small modular reactor.

Resilience

Island-capable backup

Mainspring linear generators and Hyliion KARNO units plus battery storage, sized N+1 and fuel-flexible.

Molecules

Green hydrogen & ammonia

PEM electrolysis and Haber-Bosch synthesis for fertilizer, marine fuel and industrial export.

Compute

Lake-cooled hyperscale

Deep-water heat exchange for sub-1.1 PUE, with process water returned to the lake reoxygenated.

04 · The arrival
Axis 057.81°

The campus sits beside a working runway, where the law says almost nothing may rise. So the entrance is shaped by the airspace instead of fighting it — a piece of public art that is also an FAA compliance diagram.

  1. IThe Gradient

    Eighteen pairs of anodized blades off North Marginal Road, each shorter than the last, tracing the approach surface they stand in and touching the ground exactly where it ends.

  2. IIThe Traverse

    A short, level run along the campus axis to the head of the cut.

  3. IIIThe Cut

    A 1,200-ft open processional below grade, lined by a 720-ft kinetic fin wall — 7,200 bronze blades whose rows show the campus’s live compute load. It emits no light by day.

  4. +The Line

    A 42-ft blade pair and a 1,000-ft inlaid stainless strip on the public greenway — the tallest legal object on that half of the site, given to the public rather than the front gate.

05 · Design principles

Built around the airport, the lake and the public.

The rules that shaped the drawings, and that any version of this campus would have to keep.

The airspace sets the heights

Every mass is checked against the FAA Part 77 surfaces around runway 6L/24R. Where nothing may rise, the design goes down or steps back.

The shoreline stays public

The security line runs inboard of the fire lanes, so the lakefront greenway sits outside the fence and open to everyone.

Sky between the buildings

Four discrete halls on a 550-ft pitch rather than one continuous wall, leaving 320 ft of open sky between masses from the greenway.

Safe to fly over

Amber 2200K light only with zero uplight, no open water to draw birds, and no horizontal spans for them to perch on near the approach.

The lake gets its water back

Fish-safe intake screens, a closed cooling loop through heat exchangers, and a reoxygenation cascade before anything returns to Lake Erie.

A campus people can visit

An Energy Discovery Center, a research and education cluster, and a talent pipeline from Cleveland classrooms to the plant floor.
