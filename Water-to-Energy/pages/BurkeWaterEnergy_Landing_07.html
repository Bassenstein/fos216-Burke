The Burke campus creates a five-tier educational pipeline connecting every level of Cleveland's academic ecosystem to real careers in clean energy — and uniquely, every tier uses the Burke campus itself as a live classroom. No simulation. No approximation. Real industrial hydrogen infrastructure at real operating scale.

Tier 1 · Elementary & Middle School
CMSD

Early Awareness & Environmental Literacy

Field trips from Cleveland Metropolitan School District (CMSD) elementary schools to the Energy Discovery Centre — the glass-floored observation deck above the electrolyser farm. Students in grades 3–6 watch hydrogen being made from lake water in real time, then trace it through the KARNO generator to power the lights in the building they're standing in. Curriculum modules co-developed with CMSD science teachers aligned to Ohio science standards.

  • Energy Discovery Centre field trip program
  • Ohio science standards-aligned curriculum modules
  • CMSD science teacher professional development
  • Great Lakes Science Center co-programming
Tier 2 · High School
CMSD · MC² STEM · John Hay

Two Pathways: Technician Track & Engineering Track

MC² STEM High School, John Hay STEM, and Early College High School are natural feeders. The campus creates two distinct pathways:

Technician Pathway: Junior technician apprenticeships for 10th–12th graders — 10–15 paid hours per week on-site. Students rotate through water treatment operations, electrolyser monitoring, KARNO module checks, and control room data logging. These are entry-level positions with union-negotiated wage scale, structured mentorship from licensed plant operators, and a guaranteed Tri-C interview upon graduation. Burke becomes one of the SWAG program's anchor employers.

Engineering Pathway: AP Chemistry, Physics, and Environmental Science co-taught by CMSD teachers and CWRU faculty using Burke plant data as primary source material. A student's AP Chemistry lab assignment might be analysing the purity of hydrogen output from the electrolyser. Dual enrolment via Ohio's College Credit Plus earns Tri-C credit for courses taken on or near Burke.

  • Paid junior technician apprenticeships (10–15 hr/week)
  • AP courses using live plant data as source material
  • SWAG program anchor employer status
  • College Credit Plus dual enrolment with Tri-C
Tier 3 · Community College
Cuyahoga Community College (Tri-C)

New Credentials for a New Industry

Tri-C's Workforce Innovation division — Metropolitan Campus 3 km from Burke — partners with the campus to create entirely new Ohio credentials that don't currently exist. The Burke campus creates demand for workers with skills no Ohio college currently certifies:

Electrolyser Technician Certificate: 30-credit, one-year programme combining electrochemistry fundamentals, PEM stack maintenance, water treatment operations, H₂ handling and confined space safety certification, and 200 hours of supervised plant hours at Burke. First programme of its kind in the Great Lakes region. Career starting salary: $55,000–$70,000. Journeyman wages above $80,000 after five years.

  • Electrolyser Technician Certificate (30 credits, 1 year)
  • KARNO Systems Operator credential
  • Water Treatment Technician — Electrolysis Grade
  • H₂ Safety & Handling Certification
  • 200 hours supervised plant time at Burke
  • Starting salary $55–70k · journeyman $80k+
Tier 4 · University
Cleveland State University

Engineering Co-ops & Policy Research

CSU's Energy Policy Center operates the Midwest Hydrogen Center of Excellence — covering hydrogen economy, microgrids, energy storage, electricity markets, and district energy systems. The Burke campus is the infrastructure that validates everything the RHFCC (Renewable Hydrogen Fuel Cell Collaborative, operating out of CSU) has been advocating for. CSU gains a live demonstration facility 15 minutes from its main campus.

Fenn College of Engineering BS students rotate through paid co-op placements at Burke — semester-long rotations counting toward degree requirements. Senior capstone projects tackle live engineering challenges: optimising electrolyser farm dispatch schedules, modelling district heating network thermal losses, designing protection relay settings for the microgrid islanding system.

  • CSU Midwest Hydrogen Center of Excellence anchored at Burke
  • Fenn College co-op rotations (paid, semester-long)
  • Senior capstone projects using live plant data
  • Levin College urban development & public finance research
  • Energy Policy Center hydrogen market research
Tier 5 · Research University
Case Western Reserve University

Frontier Research on a Live Industrial Scale

CWRU's Great Lakes Energy Institute was built on 80 years of electrochemistry leadership — 15 faculty members spanning 5 departments, six Electrochemical Society fellows, researching batteries, fuel cells, PEM membrane development, and electrochemical engineering. CWRU broke ground in October 2024 on a $300 million Interdisciplinary Science and Engineering Building collocating 60+ principal investigators from engineering, medicine, and sciences working on climate change, sustainable manufacturing, and materials science.

The Burke campus gives CWRU what no university lab can replicate: a full-scale, operating PEM electrolyser farm producing tens of tonnes of hydrogen per day. A PhD student investigating new PEM membrane materials installs test cells directly into the Burke electrolyser farm and collects real performance data under real operating conditions — fouling, pressure cycling, water quality variation — conditions a university lab can only approximate. The DOE awarded CWRU $10.75 million to establish a Breakthrough Electrolytes for Energy Storage research centre.

  • CWRU Clean Energy Research Institute co-operated with Cleveland Clinic
  • Live PEM test cells in operating Burke electrolyser farm
  • $300M new Interdisciplinary Science & Engineering Building (Oct 2024)
  • DOE Breakthrough Electrolytes research centre
  • Advanced manufacturing KARNO/PEM component R&D
  • Corporate R&D partnerships (high-wage employment anchor)
