01The finding that shapes everything

The last 1,200 ft of the road is zero-height

The 14 CFR Part 77 primary surface is 1,000 ft wide, centered on runway 6L/24R, and carries zero allowable height. The campus road runs at V=207 — 293 ft inside that line — from U=4,600 to U=5,173, where the primary surface ends, 200 ft past the runway end.

Over that stretch height is earned sideways only: 1 ft for every 7 ft you step lakeward of V=500, capped at 150 ft. That is what shapes the cut and what puts the one permitted vertical on the public greenway.

REV. B CORRECTION — Rev. A said this zero-height condition ran from U=4,600 to U=7,973 and that driving the gate east bought nothing. Both were wrong: the airspace model ran the primary surface east past its actual end. Beyond U=5,173 the approach surface governs and height climbs at 50:1 — 26.5 ft at U=6,500, 46.5 ft at U=7,500. The full correction and its errata are in FOS216-BEDC-010 §01 and §07.

02The proposition

Two works, one bearing

THE CUT · campus side

1,200 ft from the gate west. The road descends 12 ft over a 240-ft ramp, runs 720 ft flat between walls 90 ft apart, and climbs back out. Nothing rises above grade. From the runway, from the air, and from the lake there is nothing to see but a seam in the ground.

The north wall carries the fin field. The south wall is board-formed concrete cast with Cuyahoga Valley steel slag — the mills' waste as the wall of the thing that replaces them.

THE LINE · public side

A 42-ft blade pair on the greenway at V=802, 40 ft clear between them, and a 4-in polished stainless strip inlaid flush in the path running 1,000 ft east on 057.81°.

The blades' inner faces carry the same fin system as THE CUT, driven by the same signal. The public reads the same live campus load the staff do. Nothing is hidden from the city that is shown to the operator.

The security line was already moved inboard on BEDC-005 so the shoreline greenway stays outside the fence. THE LINE depends on that decision: it only works as a public work if the public can walk it.

04The Cut · longitudinal section

Twelve feet down, and the horizon disappears

At a 60-ft carriageway and a 12-ft wall the enclosure ratio is 1:5 — enough to feel held, not enough to feel trapped. Ramps at 5% keep the route usable by everything except heavy haul, which takes a separate at-grade route on the north side. You do not want a 400-ton generator step-up transformer coming down your front door.

05The Cut · cross section

06The Fin Wall

720 feet of the last 24 hours

Thirty-six bays, ten rows. Each bay is 40 minutes. The number of rows turned to the bright face is the campus IT load in ten steps from zero to 1,000 MW. Newest data sits at the west end, so driving inbound you move forward through the day and arrive at now as the halls come into view.

It emits no light by day. The wall reads as tone, not motion — anodized bronze turned against anodized graphite. That is the whole airport-safe argument, and it is why this is a mechanical piece rather than a screen.

07Fin module

7,200 blades on 360 drives

Twenty blades ride a common shaft per bay row. One brushless rotary with an absolute encoder turns each shaft. Torque-limited, captive pins, nothing that can leave the wall — and the cut itself shelters the whole field from wind, which is the reason a kinetic facade is defensible this close to a runway at all.

09Solstice geometry

Not designed. Found.

Runway 6L/24R was laid out in 1947 on a bearing of 057.81° true. The June 21 sunrise over Lake Erie at this latitude bears 059.0°, and the December 21 sunset bears 236.3° against a reciprocal of 237.81°. Those are misses of 1.19° and 1.51° — about two and three sun diameters.

Close enough to read as alignment standing on the path. Not close enough to call it an alignment in a document, which is why it is stated here as a near miss with the numbers attached. The campus inherits it for free; it did not earn it.

10Case A and Case B

The zero-height scheme is the permanent core

Case B — airport closed — removes the Part 77 surfaces entirely, and the campus drawn in BEDC-002 already assumes it. So strictly the entrance has no height limit. The scheme is still designed to zero, for a reason that is strategic rather than regulatory.

Closure is the one thing in this file outside your control: the FAA is on record opposed, grant obligations run to at least 2039, and the east lobe is an active federal dredge cell. A zero-height entrance is the only part of this campus buildable under either outcome — which also makes it the only part you could put on the ground now, years ahead of any closure fight, as a public lakefront amenity that asks the city for nothing.

If closure does come, the axis gains a 180-ft mast at the gate and the blades grow to 90 ft. The mast is held below 200 ft deliberately: that is the 14 CFR 77.9 notice threshold, and staying under it means no obstruction lighting on a lakefront that does not need a blinking red beacon.

11FAA constraints schedule

Height is the one everybody checks. It is not the one that kills projects.

Nine constraints govern an object this close to an active runway end. Height is the first and the most visible. Wildlife attractants and hazardous lighting are the two that most often stop a design that already cleared the height test.

ConstraintGovernsHow this scheme answers it
HEIGHT14 CFR 77.19Zero on the road for its full length; 43.1 ft at V=802. Base scheme complies. Blades at 42 ft carry 1.1 ft of margin — confirm by survey before foundations.
LIGHTINGAC 70/7460-1, AC 150/5300-13Amber 2200K only. Red, green, blue and white are aviation signal colors and cannot appear in any array near a runway end that could read as a light pattern. Grazers recessed below the top of wall, so zero uplight is guaranteed by geometry rather than by fixture optics. Photometric submittal and airspace review required.
GLAREFAA solar glare analysisAnodized and diffuse finishes only. No polished or mirror surface faces the 24R approach. The inlaid stainless strip is horizontal at V=802 and 800 ft off centerline — flag it for a glare check anyway. Any photovoltaic array triggers a full analysis.
WILDLIFEAC 150/5200-33BNo open water, no standing water beyond 48 hours, no fruit- or seed-bearing planting within 10,000 ft of the runway. This is what rules out the reflecting pool every arrival court wants. Greenway planting to a bird-hazard-screened list; the tanked slab drains to a covered sump.
FRANGIBILITY14 CFR 77.17, runway OFAThe perimeter fence runs V=170 across the compute row, which puts it inside the primary surface. At 8 ft it is an acknowledged obstruction — as is every fence at Burke today. Gate house at grade and frangible; terrace guard frangible; fence jogs to V=150 around the aperture terrace.
CONSTRUCTIONForm 7460-1, separate filingThe crane is the tallest object this project will ever have. Separate notice, night and weekend work windows, and a crane plan agreed with the tower before mobilization.
NAVAID / RADARShadow studyThe fin wall sits 12 ft below grade with no line of sight to the ASR or any NAVAID. The Case B mast requires a shadow study and its own 7460-1.
FODPart 139 airfield conditionEvery kinetic component captive and torque-limited. No loose fasteners within the runway object free area. The cut shelters the fin field from wind, which is what makes the moving parts defensible here.
THERMALPlume and turbulenceNo plume from any part of the arrival works. Generation is air-cooled, the lake loop is closed, and nothing in the gateway rejects heat.

13What the projector caught

Five errors the plans did not show

Every view in BEDC-008 is a true pinhole projection of this geometry, not an illustration. That is worth the trouble because it fails loudly when the design is wrong.

CaughtWhat happened
The aperture did not workIt was first drawn as a ha-ha — ground sloping up out of the cut. From eye 5 ft 6 in on a floor 12 ft down, the slope crests above the sight line at 5.2° against a 2.1° view angle, and hides the runway it exists to reveal. AR-4 came back empty. Redrawn as a terrace at grade reached by ten treads.
A fence post in the shotThe standard perimeter runs V=170 across the compute row, which put an 8-ft post 12 ft in front of the aperture camera, and posts land every 45 ft from U=4,470 — one of them exactly at U=4,560. The fence jogs to V=150 around the terrace and the camera moved to U=4,537.
A lens that could not hold its subjectAR-5 at 28 mm could not fit hall 4's 120-ft parapet from 230 ft. Corrected to 20 mm and the camera swung 60 ft north off the wall.
An impossible cameraAR-8's first position put the heat-exchange plant's north face within a few feet of the lens, which blanketed the frame and hid the mast entirely. Camera relocated west of the plant.
The runway was never modeledNo earlier view in this set looked south off the property, so 6L/24R had never been drawn. AR-4 does, and needed it built.

Closing

What this fixes

BEDC-005 ended by naming a real gap: 2,600 ft of empty road to a gate, with no address and no forecourt. A council member or a tenant notices that in the first thirty seconds. This sheet answers it without asking the FAA for anything, and it does so by taking the one thing the airspace actually permits — a 43-ft vertical on public ground — and giving it to the public rather than to the front gate.

Open items: price the tanked slab before adopting the cut; survey the runway centerline before committing to 42 ft on the greenway; and agree the fin wall data mapping with operations once the first hall is commissioned and real load exists.

Read with FOS216-BEDC-010, which carries the east gate, the gradient, the corrected airspace model and the errata this revision depends on.
