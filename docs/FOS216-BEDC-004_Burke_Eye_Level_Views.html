Every view on this sheet is a real pinhole projection of the BEDC-002 geometry — 36 × 24 mm sensor, stated focal length, camera at 5 ft 6 in, verticals vertical, no tilt and no artistic license. Nothing is sketched. The horizon line in each frame is the true horizon for that eye height, and every mass sits where the plan puts it.

What this sheet is for

An image model given only a plan and a massing axonometric has to guess the eye-level composition, and it guesses badly — it compresses spacing, it exaggerates height, it puts the horizon in the wrong place. These six views are the missing reference: attach the one matching the shot, and the generator is being shown the frame rather than asked to infer it. They are geometry, not renderings — no materials, no light, no weather. That is deliberate.

01 Method & corrections

A real camera, and what it caught

Each view is built by transforming the world geometry into camera space, projecting through a pinhole at the stated focal length, clipping to the near plane and painting faces far to near with back-face culling. The image plane is 3:2. Nothing is adjusted after projection, which is why the framing is sometimes awkward — that is what the camera sees.

The point of doing it this way

Four of six prompts named a lens that could not hold the subject, and two put the camera somewhere a person cannot stand. None of that is visible from a plan or an axonometric — it only appears when you actually project the geometry through a camera. BEDC-003 Rev. B carries the corrections.

08 Using these as references

Two images, one sentence, then the prompt

Attach the eye-level view first and the plan second. Nano Banana weights earlier attachments more heavily for composition, and composition is exactly what this sheet supplies.

ShotAttach 1 · compositionAttach 2 · layoutSay this first
1GL-1SP-1“Image 1 is an eye-level perspective massing study of the scene. Image 2 is the site plan. Match image 1’s camera, framing and building positions exactly.”
2GL-2SP-1Same, plus “keep the gaps between buildings exactly as wide as image 1 shows them.”
3GL-3SP-2Same, plus “the large wall fills the frame as in image 1.”
4GL-4SP-3Same, plus “the two thin vertical stacks stay at the height shown.”
5GL-5SP-3Same, plus “the plant is a low horizontal band, not a tower.”
6GL-6SP-1Same, plus “the campus occupies only the narrow band above the horizon that image 1 shows.”

What to say about the colors

  • These views are flat-shaded massing in the FOS216 palette. Teal is not a material and orange is not a material
  • Tell the generator: “Image 1 is a massing study, not a color reference. Use the materials described in the text.” without that line it will render teal buildings
  • The dark background is not night. Say so if the model keeps returning night scenes

What these views deliberately omit

  • No materials, no texture, no panel joints — those live in the finish schedule on BEDC-002 §10
  • No sun, no shadows, no sky — the light comes from the prompt, and each shot has its own
  • No people except the scale figures, no vehicles, no vegetation detail
  • No terrain relief. The site is flat fill at elevation 584 and is drawn that way

If the render still fights you

Crop the eye-level view to just the buildings and attach that instead of the full sheet. The horizon line, the dimension leaders and the labels are useful to a person and are noise to a generator — more than once they come back drawn into the render as graphics. When that happens, crop tighter and say “ignore any lines or text in the reference image; they are drafting annotation.”
