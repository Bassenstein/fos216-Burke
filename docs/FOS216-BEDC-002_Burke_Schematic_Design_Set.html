Status of this set

This is schematic design against a traced boundary. Nothing here is survey, nothing is engineered, and nothing establishes site control. Every building footprint, road, yard and utility route is a study geometry chosen to test fit — dimensions are drawn to the foot because a drawing has to be, not because anything has been verified. The Part 77 surfaces are modeled, not determined: they show where an FAA Form 7460-1 study would be required and how it would likely come out. Read this set with FOS216-BEDC-001 Rev. 01 Basis & Datum

One frame, ten drawings, and a north arrow that is not straight up

Every sheet in this set uses the same coordinate system, so a point on SP-1 is the same point on SP-7. U runs along the runway heading from the west tip of the parcel; V runs perpendicular from the 6L/24R centerline, positive toward the lake. Plan up is therefore 327.8° true, not north — true north sits 32.2° clockwise from plan up, and every north arrow in this set is rotated to match.

SP-4 Utilities, Circulation & the Lake Loop

The cooling answer a lakefront site is actually for

Every earlier FOS216 site rejected heat to dry coolers because it had no alternative. Burke does. The compute row runs a closed glycol loop to titanium plate exchangers in a heat exchange plant at U = 4,250, and the exchangers are cooled by a deep-water intake past the breakwater with a reoxygenating diffuser outfall. Nothing evaporates, nothing goes to NEORSD, and there is no dry cooler field — which matters, because at this load a dry cooler field would run 25 to 35 acres and no earlier sheet in this file ever drew one.

Say the flow number out loud

530 million gallons a day is the honest consequence of putting a gigawatt of liquid-cooled compute on a lake. It is also the reason the two water criteria on the Preferred Development scorecard exist, and the reason the intake has to be deep and the diffuser has to reoxygenate. A program that shows a lake loop and does not show this number is doing what MLP Ventures did with generation in Upper Merion. Put it on the first page of the filing.

The massing decision worth defending

Four discrete halls on a 550-ft pitch instead of one continuous building costs roughly a tenth of the structure and circulation efficiency, and it is the right trade. From the greenway, 320 ft of sky between masses is the difference between a lakefront and a wall — and the wall is the image the opposition would otherwise be handed for free.

Render these conditions

  • Summer sunrise, 6:00 a.m. — camera 1, sun on the axis at about 1° altitude. The greenway image
  • Equinox mid-morning, 9:30 a.m. — camera 2, sun at roughly 30° and 125° azimuth, raking the north faces off the water
  • Winter sunset, 5:00 p.m. — camera 5, stacks against a low sun down the axis. The hardest image and the one worth getting right
  • Overcast, 11,000 ft ceiling — camera 6. Cleveland is overcast more than half the year; an all-golden-hour set is a tell

Atmosphere notes

  • Lake haze off the water almost every summer morning; keep aerial perspective heavy past 3,000 ft
  • Prevailing wind is out of the southwest — ACC and stack plumes drift toward the lake, away from the city
  • No visible plume from either the ACC or the lake loop; if the render shows steam, the render is wrong
  • Lake-effect snow is a real winter condition here. One snow frame reads as local knowledge
  • Ice on the East Basin from roughly January to March; open water at the intake head year round

One rule for every render in this set

Nothing here may be rendered with signage, branding, a tenant logo, or a name on a building. There is no tenant, no lease and no site control, and a render that implies otherwise is exactly the document that gets quoted back at a hearing. Keep every surface generic.

11 Departures & Open Items

What this set changes, and what it still cannot answer

Departures from the module standard

  • Cooling. MPDT and the Opportunity Corridor reject heat to dry coolers. Burke rejects to the lake through plate exchangers. That is a different machine, a different permit path and a different failure mode — and it is the only version that earns the two water criteria on the scorecard.
  • The dry cooler field was never drawn. On the inland sheets the "cooling plant" is a 235 × 160 CDU block. At 300 MW of rejection per hall the dry cooler field alone is 6 to 9 acres, and it appears on no sheet in this file. Burke has room for it; the Opportunity Corridor, at 34.3% coverage, did not. That is a finding against the earlier sheets, not against this one.
  • No acoustic berm on the public frontage. The FOS216 standard puts a 20-ft berm on every public edge. Here the public edge faces the lake, and a 20-ft berm would wall the greenway off from the water it exists for. The halls themselves screen the south. The berm standard is right for a boulevard and wrong for a shoreline.
  • Generation is air-cooled, not lake-cooled. Adding the combined cycle's condensing duty to the lake loop would push the intake past 800 MGD. The ACC costs efficiency and buys a defensible number.

Open items this set does not close

  • Geotechnical. Placed dredge fill on a lake bottom. Deep foundations or ground improvement are certain, differential settlement under a 120-ft eight-story hall is the governing design problem, and none of it is priced or drawn.
  • The CDF cap. The east lobe is USACE CDF No. 10B. Cell closure, cap design and the placement schedule govern the energy block completely, and the answer may be decades out.
  • Gas. Source, route, pressure, capacity and the cost of getting a high-pressure lateral to a lakefront peninsula. Entirely unstudied and potentially fatal to the two-block scheme.
  • Intake and outfall permitting. ODNR submerged lands, USACE Section 10, Ohio EPA thermal, Clean Water Act §316(b). The 530 MGD figure makes every one of these a major action, not a minor one.
  • Nearshore current direction at the intake and diffuser. If the current runs the wrong way the two structures short-circuit and the whole loop has to be re-laid.
  • Access. Every vehicle on this campus arrives across the former airfield. If the airport closes but the airfield is not conveyed, the site has no legal access at all.
  • Bird strike and habitat. Dike 10B is a documented wildlife attractant. The greenway on SP-1 makes that better for the public and possibly worse for the airport — which cuts both ways in a closure argument.
  • FAA Form 7460-1. Every height figure in this set is modeled from published standards. File the determinations before anything else in this set is relied on.
