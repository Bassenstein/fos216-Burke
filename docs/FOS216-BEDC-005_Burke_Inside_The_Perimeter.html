inside the perimeter,

BEDC-004 stood outside and looked in. This sheet walks the campus — through the gate, down the service side, into the gap between two halls, along the fire lane at the public edge, then east into the plant and finally under the condenser deck. Same U-V frame, same pinhole projector, same rule that the camera never tilts.

One thing changed on the plan to make this sheet possible

Drawing the perimeter revealed that a fence on the property line puts the shoreline greenway inside the secure area, which would make the public edge private and gut the whole civic argument. The security line has been moved inboard — V = 850 across the compute row and V = 1,150 across the energy block, just outside the fire lanes. The greenway and its trail now sit outside the fence where they belong. Everything on this sheet is drawn to that line; SP-1 on BEDC-002 shows the fence at the property line and is superseded on this point.

Five things that were not visible from a plan

#FindingWhereWhat to do
1A fence on the property line makes the public greenway private. The security perimeter has to run inboard of it.Whole siteFixed on this sheet — V = 850 west, V = 1,150 east. Carry it back into SP-1.
2A level camera cannot hold a 120-ft parapet inside about 190 ft at 20 mm, or 285 ft at 28 mm.IN-3Never brief a close hall render without a stated stand-off distance, or you will get a tilted camera and a building that leans.
3Hall 3’s transformer bay sits in the only clear approach to its west wall. Same condition on all four halls.IN-3Either accept it in every close frame or move the GSU bays to the south face. A design decision, not a rendering one.
4The gap between the two combined cycle blocks is 50 ft. That is the tightest dimension anywhere on the campus.IN-9Check against fire access, crane laydown and HRSG module delivery before the next revision.
5The condenser deck is the only covered space on the site — 320 by 520 ft at 108 ft clear.IN-10Worth drawing properly. It is the most structurally interesting thing on the campus and appears on no earlier sheet.

The arrival sequence is the weak one

Stop 1 is 2,600 ft of empty road. There is no address, no forecourt, no arrival building and nothing to look at until the halls resolve at half a mile. That is a real gap in the scheme, not a rendering problem — and it is the frame a visiting tenant or a council member would actually see first. Worth a small arrival building and a turnaround at the west side of the gate before this goes into a deck.

Detail added at this range

  • Perimeter fence, 8 ft, posts at 45 ft — drawn because it is the subject at half these stations
  • Condenser modeled as an open column grid with a 12-ft deck, instead of the solid box on BEDC-002 and BEDC-004
  • Dock doors, stair cores and louver bands on the hall faces
  • Bus runs on the switchyard roof

Still deliberately absent

  • No materials, no texture, no color that means anything — flat massing only
  • No sun, no shadow, no sky. The light comes from the prompt
  • No vehicles, no equipment, no pipe racks, no cable tray
  • Volumes match BEDC-002 exactly; only the level of detail changed
