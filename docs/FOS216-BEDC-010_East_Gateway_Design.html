THE GRADIENT

You come off the north side of North Marginal Road heading due north onto the peninsula at 41.525168, -81.668573 — the existing service road, and the last land on the parcel's south edge before the East 55th basin.

Eighteen pairs of anodized blades run west from the turn on a 65:1 plane, each pair shorter than the last, tracing the approach surface that governs them. They reach the ground exactly where the primary surface begins — and that is where the road drops into the cut.

The airspace is not a constraint the design works around. It is the form.

REV. C — Rev. A/B sited this gate at U=7,300. Checked against the source aerial, that point is 1,255 ft out into the marina basin. The gate is corrected to U=5,950, the envelope from 42.5 ft to 15.5 ft, and the row from 36 pairs to 18. See §07.

01The correction

My airspace model was wrong east of U=5,173

The previous version of h_limit() took the minimum of a transitional surface and the approach surface — and because the transitional function returned zero for anything within 500 ft of the centerline at every station, it ran the primary surface east forever and reported zero allowable height for the full length of the parcel.

The primary surface ends 200 ft past the runway end, at U=5,173. Beyond it the approach surface governs inside its flaring trapezoid, and the transitional rises 7:1 off the approach edge — which is itself already climbing.

02The gate

Where the drive comes in

Entry at U=5,950 on the parcel’s south boundary, at the service road already running onto the peninsula between the runway end and the marina basin. You come north off North Marginal, pass the arrival pair, clear the barrier and turn west.

The turn matters. The gradient row straddles the spine road, which runs east-west, because the approach plane descends with distance west. Approaching from the south you would pass that row sideways — which AR-10 caught. So a second pair, 10 ft, straddles the drive itself 200 ft back from the turn, giving the northward approach its own threshold.

From the turn it is 1,990 ft west to the head of the cut and 3,750 ft to hall 1.

03The gradient

Eighteen pairs on a 65:1 plane

Blade height is (U − 5,173) / 65 — a plane parallel to the 50:1 approach surface and 23% below it. One rule sets every blade and states the margin as a single number. The tallest is 12 ft at the turn; the shortest is 4 ft, below which a blade stops reading and the row simply ends.

Why 65:1 and not the 58:1 of Rev. A/B: the margin is proportional to the run, and over 517 ft instead of 1,895 a 58:1 plane leaves only 2.1 ft at the tallest blade. That is too thin against a runway centerline we have not surveyed. 65:1 gives 3.5 ft.

Eighteen pairs against thirty-six fin-wall bays — one pair for every two bays.

04Threshold pair

One moment of the instrument, then silence

Only the threshold pair carries a fin field. The other seventeen, and the arrival pair on the drive, are plain anodized aluminum. Repeating the campus-load instrument on every blade would turn a sentence into wallpaper — and the instrument has 720 ft of wall further west where it can actually say something.

08Looking north

What the campus looks like from the road

Every other view in this set looks west down the axis or east along the greenway. These two look due north, which is the direction the site is actually approached and seen from.

The honest result: from North Marginal, 930 ft back across the airfield, the campus barely registers. A 120-ft block at 1,300 ft occupies a fifth of the frame at a normal lens, and what fills the view is the energy block, not the compute halls — those sit far to the left. Whether that is a good outcome depends on the argument being made. For a lakefront the city is protective of, low visual impact is a defense. For a project that wants presence, it is a problem.

10FAA notes for the east gate

Nothing penetrates. Almost everything still needs filing.

No blade in the row penetrates the approach surface — the tallest clears by 5.8 ft. But penetration is not what triggers notice.

14 CFR 77.9(b) requires a Form 7460-1 filing for anything exceeding a 100:1 slope measured from the nearest point of the nearest runway, out to 20,000 ft, for runways longer than 3,200 ft. Burke 6L/24R is 6,198 ft, so the 100:1 rule applies — and it is far more restrictive out here than the approach surface is.

This is notice, not prohibition. Since nothing penetrates a Part 77 surface, a “no hazard” determination is the expected outcome — but the filing is mandatory, it covers 12 of the 18 gradient pairs plus both arrival blades, and it should be made as one submission for the whole installation rather than blade by blade.

ItemNote
SURVEY FIRSTThe envelope is measured from the 6L/24R centerline, and the centerline position is the one dimension on this site taken from published geometry rather than from the ground. Survey it before any foundation is poured.
NO SPANA portal beam at 36 ft is the obvious gesture and the wrong one: a horizontal member near a runway approach is a perch, and AC 150/5200-33B does not want perches within 10,000 ft. Two free-standing piers do the same work.
LIGHTINGAmber only, shielded, downward. No obstruction lighting is required below 200 ft and none should be added voluntarily — a blinking red beacon on the lakefront is exactly what this design is trying not to be.
FRANGIBILITYAt 1,000 ft from the runway end the row is outside the object free area, so standard structural design applies. Confirm against the current ALP.
GLAREAnodized and diffuse. The blades face the road, not the approach.
